# v0.0.5-hazards — native original-clock hazards diagnostic

This prerelease adds original-clock threat, fish and shore/bear collision acquisition to the native MSX1 core. Row attachment/pushing, fish segment removal and delayed rewards, bear refuge/chase, water/temperature transitions and RNG ordering now use the original collision channels. This is a diagnostic step toward the complete port.

- ROM: `frostbite-0.0.5-hazards.rom`, 4,194,304 bytes, **ASCII16**.
- SHA-256: `37beb4d58bfc6566ba064ff59d48118ce6bc70655f92dcc9695cb534a88bfd93`.
- Build source: [`e83569d`](https://github.com/sandersouza/msxgp-frostbite/commit/e83569dfde46f9bb6f39ea05817d6e3204333a54).
- Target: standard-speed MSX1, 64 KiB RAM, 16 KiB TMS9918-family VRAM, built-in PSG. No expansion, turbo, disk or additional sound hardware is required. This diagnostic is silent.

## Current checks and limits

Automated acceptance on an isolated C-BIOS MSX1 covers 50,289,350 original-clock source cells, 16,776,480 independent physical-ROM leaf queries, 14,828 complete visible-phase comparisons and 652 controlled full-native-phase cases. All 65,536 actual-Z80 shift/flag cases pass. The shared baseline translator and published 0.0.4 ROM identity are preserved.

Four cold input-only original routes and independent full-state replays cover fish collection with all twenty delayed ten-point awards, threat attachment/push/death/recovery, bear chase/recovery and the left refuge. The actual MSX ROM matches every logical CPU register and all 128 RAM bytes in all 32 route × NTSC/PAL × layout × width combinations. These add 24,604 completed frames checked pixel by pixel, with no canonical state/contact injection or dropped source intervals. Fifteen dense/cache/toggle stress scenarios add 3,600 frames; eleven intentional faults are detected.

The tightest named-route PAL margin is 645 cycles (70,719/71,364); the busiest named NTSC frame uses 47,558/59,736. Crowded stress peaks at 49,545 NTSC and 64,802 PAL cycles. VRAM spacing stays at least 29 cycles; sprite occupancy, VBlank commits, guards, slots and interrupts pass. Earlier timing failures were rejected and the changed ROM received fresh full acceptance.

Graphics and HUD remain synthetic, igloo progress is coarse and audio is pending. Score, temperature and reserves are not fully displayed; their source-state transitions are checked automatically. Presentation clipping/art and whole-game/progression acceptance remain later work. Automated evidence does not claim manual-emulator or physical MSX acceptance.

## Human tests requested

1. Load the attached ROM with mapper **ASCII16** on an ordinary 64 KiB MSX1 or equivalent emulator at normal speed. Try NTSC and PAL when available. Record machine/BIOS/VDP, emulator version, mapper and whether this is emulator or physical hardware.
2. Press/release F1 to start/reset. Arrows or joystick move and jump; Space or joystick fire reverses the contacted blue row on a fresh press. F2 cycles the original variations; keyboard controls follow the active player and joysticks use ports 1/2. F3 changes diagnostic layout and F4 changes width once per press; try all four combinations, especially PAL.
3. Collect a fish while grounded on a floe. Expect one fish segment to disappear and the original short reward pause. The complete 200-point delayed envelope is verified automatically; the diagnostic HUD does not display the complete score. Jump across threats: airborne overlap should not attach the player. Grounded threat contact should attach/push along its row and release or lead to the original death/recovery sequence.
4. From a cold start, press/release F2 twice, then F1, for an advanced start with a bear. Reach the extreme-left shore edge and wait; expect the bear to stop short of that refuge. Reset and wait nearer the bear's path: expect a grounded hit, leftward chase/push and eventual death/recovery. In a two-player variation, confirm control follows the active player after a lost reserve.
5. Continue jumping, building and reversing rows while switching F3/F4. Expect stable pacing and presentation, including during collection/death/recovery. Record reproducible inputs and video for unexpected contact, attachment, fish removal, refuge behavior or pacing; include the selected variation and F3/F4 settings.

Silence, synthetic art and the limited HUD are expected. Send actual observations and any emulator/physical differences; those feedback classes remain separate from automated checks.
